The Lawn Care Customers You Lose Every Winter

Connor Bolin · Sep 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Lawn care customers rarely cancel in any dramatic way. The season winds down, the weekly visits stop, and everyone assumes service will pick back up in spring. Then spring arrives, the phones go crazy with new requests, and nobody circles back to the customers who were quietly paused since October. Some of them sign with whichever company happens to call first when the grass starts growing.

We covered the quote-request side of the business in AI for Landscaping & Lawn Care Companies in Raleigh, but keeping the customers you already have deserves its own math, because a returning customer costs far less than a new one.

The math most companies haven't run

Here's an example of what winter drift actually costs:

  • Say a landscaping company has 250 recurring maintenance customers averaging about $1,400 a season.
  • If 15% of them (roughly 38 customers) don't return the next spring because nobody reached out to confirm their schedule, that's about $52,500 in recurring revenue gone, before counting any mulch, aeration, or add-on jobs those customers would have booked.
  • Replacing them means winning 38 new customers during the busiest, most competitive weeks of the year, when every other company is chasing the same homeowners.

Run your own numbers with your customer count and average season value. The exact figures vary, but the pattern holds: the customers you already have are the cheapest revenue you'll ever earn.

Why customers lapse over the off-season

It's a timing problem, not a service problem. Crews are laid off or cut back in winter, the office is quiet, and renewal conversations get pushed to "when things pick up." Then things pick up all at once, and the team is buried in new quote requests and route scheduling. A homeowner who was happy in October has heard from three other companies by April, and the one who reaches out first often wins the season.

What actually works: renewal check-ins timed to the calendar

The fix is a renewal sequence that runs on its own schedule. A friendly end-of-season message goes out in the fall to say thanks and preview what next season looks like. In late winter, before the rush, each customer gets a one-tap link to confirm their spring schedule. Customers who still haven't replied a few weeks past their usual start date get a gentle nudge, and the same system can offer seasonal add-ons like aeration, overseeding, or mulch installs at the right time. It works with software companies already use, such as LMN and Jobber, so it runs off the customer list you already keep. It's the same idea as the maintenance plan renewals we wrote about for HVAC, tuned to a seasonal business.

Where we draw a hard line

Everything here handles renewal check-ins, reminders, and scheduling. It never sets prices, changes a contract, or makes on-the-ground work decisions, and crew routing stays with your team. The goal is making sure every returning customer hears from you before a competitor does, not replacing any part of the work done in the yard.

The bottom line

A landscaping company doesn't need to win more customers every spring, it usually needs to keep the ones it already has. That's a timing and follow-up problem, and it has a simple fix.
