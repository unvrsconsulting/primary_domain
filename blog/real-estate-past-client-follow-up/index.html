The Past-Client List Most Real Estate Agents Never Work

Connor Bolin · Sep 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Most real estate marketing is aimed at strangers: ads, open houses, portal leads. Meanwhile the cheapest source of business an agent will ever have is already in their phone, the people they've already helped buy or sell a home. Those clients trust them, remember the experience, and know other people who are about to move. Yet for most agents, contact drops off almost completely a few weeks after closing.

We covered the speed side of the business in How Raleigh Real Estate Teams Never Lose a Lead to Speed, but the past-client list deserves its own math, because it's the one lead source that costs nothing to acquire.

The math most agents haven't run

Here's an example of what a neglected past-client list actually costs:

  • Say an agent has 200 past clients in their CRM. Some will move again, and many more know someone who will.
  • If staying in touch produces just 3 extra referrals or repeat deals a year, at a net commission of around $9,000 a deal, that's $27,000 a year from people the agent has already served.
  • Winning three brand-new deals through paid leads costs real advertising spend and a lot of early-stage effort. A past client who thinks of you first costs a text message.

Run your own numbers with your list size and average commission. The exact figures vary widely, but the pattern is consistent: the list is worth far more than the time most agents spend on it.

Why the list goes cold

It isn't that agents don't care. Once a deal closes, the urgent work is always the next deal: the new lead that just came in, the showing this weekend, the offer due tomorrow. Nobody owns "stay in touch with people from two years ago," so it never gets scheduled. And the outreach that works has to feel personal, which is exactly why it takes more time than an agent has on a busy week.

What actually works: a light, personal-feeling sequence that runs on its own

The fix is a small number of well-timed touches per year, sent in the agent's own voice: a check-in around the anniversary of closing, a seasonal note or local market update the agent has approved, and an easy way for the client to reply. When a client answers, whether they mention they're thinking of selling or that a friend is looking, the reply routes straight to the agent so a real conversation starts while the interest is fresh. It runs through tools agents already use, like Follow Up Boss or GoHighLevel, so the list stays in one place. It's the same discipline behind fast lead follow-up, pointed at people who already know you.

Where we draw a hard line

Texts go only to clients who have agreed to receive them, with an easy way to opt out. The system handles staying in touch and routing replies, nothing more. It never gives pricing or valuation opinions, negotiates, or touches contract and legal matters, those stay entirely with the agent. The goal is making sure a past client hears from you at all, not replacing the relationship.

The bottom line

A real estate agent doesn't need a bigger ad budget to grow, it usually needs the people they've already helped to hear from them a few times a year.
