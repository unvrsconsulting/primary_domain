Restaurant Phone Automation: What to Hand Off, What to Keep

Connor Bolin · Nov 15, 2026 · 3 min read

A restaurant's phone gets a mix of calls, and they aren't equally easy to hand off. Some are the same three questions asked all day. Others, like a guest asking about a food allergy, need a person who knows the kitchen. Knowing which is which is the whole job of setting up phone automation well.

We cover the rush-hour side in AI for restaurants in the Triangle. This post is about sorting the calls themselves.

The calls a restaurant actually gets

  • Hours, address, and parking. The same answers, over and over.
  • Reservations. Party size, date, time, and a name and number.
  • Takeout orders and order status. Fast, repetitive, and time-sensitive.
  • Menu questions. Some simple, some detailed.
  • Catering and private events. Higher value, more back-and-forth.
  • Allergy and dietary questions. Safety-related, and not routine.
  • Complaints and lost items. Emotional, and they need a human touch.

What a phone assistant can take

The first four are good candidates: hours and directions, reservations, takeout orders, and simple menu questions. A well-built assistant answers on the first ring, collects the details, and passes reservations and orders into your system or to your team, so the host can stay with the guests at the door. It can also text a confirmation. Details vary by the tools a restaurant already uses, so the setup should start from what you have.

What to keep with your staff

  • Allergy and dietary questions. Guests are relying on the answer for their health. An assistant shouldn't guess, and it shouldn't reassure. It should hand the call to someone who can check with the kitchen, or take a message and have a person call back.
  • Complaints. A guest who had a bad night wants a person.
  • Large parties and events. Pricing, menus, and deposits are a conversation.
  • Anything the assistant isn't sure about. Handing off is better than improvising.

Set it up around your own rules

Write down your hours, your reservation policy, how takeout works, and exactly which questions go to a person. Include a clear line for allergies. If you're not sure how to structure the handoff, our post on teaching your AI to hand off shows how.
