How Much Does an AI Receptionist Cost? A 2026 Raleigh Pricing Guide

Connor Bolin · Sep 14, 2026 · 6 min read

"How much does an AI receptionist cost" is one of the most common searches we see from Raleigh business owners, and most of the answers online are either vague ("contact us for pricing") or written by a company trying to upsell you before you understand the basics. Here's the plain-numbers version.

The short answer

Most small businesses in the Triangle end up somewhere between $150 and $600 a month for a fully built AI receptionist, plus a one-time setup fee that typically runs $500 to $2,500 depending on how much custom work the build requires. A single-location service business with straightforward hours, pricing, and booking logic lands toward the low end. A multi-location operation with complex scheduling rules, multiple call types, and deep CRM integration lands toward the high end.

What actually drives the price

The monthly number moves based on a handful of concrete factors, not vague "tiers":

  • Call volume. Most platforms bill per minute of talk time above a base allotment. A business fielding 200 calls a month costs less to run than one fielding 2,000.
  • Integration depth. Reading availability from Google Calendar and texting a lead is simple. Writing appointments directly into ServiceTitan, Jobber, or a practice management system with real-time double-booking checks is a heavier build.
  • Number of call flows. A receptionist that only books appointments is simpler than one that also handles billing questions, dispatches emergencies, and routes VIP callers differently.
  • Ongoing tuning. Scripts and edge cases get refined after launch based on real call transcripts. Some of that is one-time; some businesses want it as an ongoing service.

AI receptionist vs. a traditional answering service

A human answering service typically runs $300 to $800 a month for coverage that's often inconsistent, reads from a generic script, and has no real context about your business. We covered why that gap matters so much in The Real Cost of a Missed Call. An AI receptionist usually lands in a similar or lower monthly range, but answers in one or two rings every time, knows your actual services and pricing, and never puts a caller on hold.

What's typically included, and what costs extra

A standard build usually includes: call answering and routing, appointment booking synced to your calendar, basic FAQ handling (hours, pricing ranges, service area), and lead texts or emails sent in real time. What usually costs extra: minutes beyond your monthly allotment, deep integrations with niche industry software, multi-language support, and ongoing script tuning beyond the first round after launch. Ask any vendor to break down exactly which of these are included before signing anything, since "AI receptionist" means very different things depending on who's building it.

Is it worth it for your business?

The math is usually simple: if your business is losing even a handful of jobs a month to unanswered calls, a setup in this price range pays for itself well inside the first month it's live. The bottom line

AI receptionist pricing isn't complicated once you know what drives it: call volume, integration depth, and how many call flows you actually need. Most Raleigh small businesses land in the $150–$600 a month range with a modest setup fee, and the system typically pays for itself fast.
