Veterinary Clinic Phones: What AI Can Take, What It Can't

Connor Bolin · Nov 18, 2026 · 3 min read

A vet clinic's phone carries two very different kinds of calls. Some are routine: book a booster, ask about hours, request a refill. Others come from someone who's worried about an animal right now. A good phone setup treats them differently, and the line between them is what matters most.

An important note: an AI receptionist handles scheduling and administrative communication only. It doesn't give medical advice, assess symptoms, or make clinical decisions. Those stay with your veterinary team.

The calls a clinic gets

  • Booking and rescheduling appointments.
  • Hours, location, and new-client questions.
  • Prescription refill requests.
  • Records and vaccine certificate requests.
  • Questions about test results.
  • Symptoms and "is this an emergency?" calls.
  • Sensitive calls, including end-of-life conversations.

What an AI receptionist can take

The administrative ones: booking, rescheduling, hours and directions, new-client details, and the intake side of refill and records requests. For a refill, that means collecting the pet's name, the medication, and the owner's contact details, then passing the request to your team. It doesn't decide anything. It also frees your front desk from the phone so they can focus on the animals and owners in the building.

What must go to your team

  • Anything about symptoms or how a pet is acting. The assistant shouldn't interpret it, reassure, or guess.
  • Emergencies. The caller should be told to reach your emergency line or the nearest emergency animal hospital, per the rules you set.
  • Medication and dosage questions.
  • Test results.
  • Sensitive and emotional calls. A grieving owner needs a person, not a script.

Write your emergency rule first

Before anything else, decide what the phone should say when a caller describes a possible emergency. Many clinics refer after-hours emergencies to a dedicated hospital, and some have a clinician on call. Whatever your rule is, write it down and test it.
