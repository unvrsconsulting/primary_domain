Payment Reminder Messages: What to Say and When to Send

Connor Bolin · Nov 11, 2026 · 3 min read

Most late invoices aren't from customers who refuse to pay. They were forgotten, buried in an inbox, or waiting on someone else to approve them. A good payment reminder fixes that without making anyone feel accused, and it doesn't take much: the right words, sent at the right time.

A note before we start: this is general information, not legal advice. Rules about collecting debts and sending texts depend on the situation and the state. See our consent checklist for texting, and check with an attorney about collections.

A simple reminder schedule

  • A few days before the due date: a friendly heads-up with the amount and a payment link.
  • On the due date: a short note that payment is due today.
  • 3 to 7 days after: a polite "this may have slipped through" message.
  • Two weeks or more after: a firmer message, and a phone call from a person.
  • Beyond that: a decision for you, not for software.

Three example messages

These are templates. The business, names and amounts are made up, so adapt them to your own voice.

Before the due date: "Hi Maria, a quick reminder from Riverside Cleaning that invoice 1042 for $180 is due Friday. You can pay here: [link]. Thank you!"

Just past due: "Hi Maria, this may have slipped through. Invoice 1042 for $180 was due on Friday. You can pay here: [link]. If something's off, just reply and we'll sort it out."

Firmer follow-up: "Hi Maria, we haven't received payment for invoice 1042 ($180), now two weeks past due. Could you pay today or let us know what's happening? I'm happy to talk it through: [phone number]."

Make paying easy

Every reminder should include the amount, the invoice number, a direct payment link, and a person to contact. Friction is a common reason for delays, and a link someone can tap on their phone removes most of it.

What to avoid

  • Threats and pressure. They damage the relationship and can create legal problems.
  • Shaming or mentioning the debt to anyone else. Keep it between you and the customer.
  • Fees you haven't agreed to. Only mention late charges that are in your terms.
  • Too many messages. A steady rhythm beats a flood.
  • Reminders after it's been paid. Check the status before each one goes out.

What to automate and what to keep human

The reminders and payment links are good candidates for automation, since they're repetitive and time-sensitive. Disputes, hardship, and large accounts should go to a person. The bottom line

Reminders work best when they're early, polite, and easy to act on. Start with a friendly message before the due date and keep the tone calm until a person needs to step in. To check it's working, track how many invoices get paid on time before and after, using our 5 numbers to check.
