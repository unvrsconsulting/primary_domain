Your Busy Season Is When the Phone Fails Most

Connor Bolin · Oct 6, 2026 · 6 min read

Most of what we write about is the quiet, everyday leak: a call missed here, a quote forgotten there. Busy season is different. It's the stretch when demand spikes all at once, the phone rings nonstop, and the same team that handles a normal week comfortably can't keep up with a heat wave, a spring lawn rush, a holiday weekend, or a cold snap that breaks half the furnaces in town.

The painful part is the timing. The weeks when customers are most ready to book are the weeks you're least able to answer. We covered the everyday version in the real cost of a missed call. This is what that looks like when volume triples.

The math most businesses haven't run

Here's an illustrative example, so swap in your own numbers:

  • Say your office can comfortably handle about 60 calls a day. During a ten-day heat wave, calls jump to 150 a day.
  • That's about 90 calls a day your team can't reach, or 900 over ten days.
  • Say a third of those are genuine new-booking requests (the rest are existing customers, vendors, and wrong numbers), and 40% of those would have booked. That's about 120 jobs.
  • At an average ticket of $300, that's roughly $36,000 in ten days, during the exact window when your technicians are busiest and your prices are least negotiable.

Your volumes and ticket size will differ. The shape won't: a business at capacity can't take more work by answering the phone harder, so overflow simply goes to whoever picks up next.

Why hiring for the rush rarely works

The obvious fix is seasonal help, and sometimes it's the right call. But a temporary hire needs training before they're useful, which usually means they're only ready for the back half of a rush that lasted two weeks. They also cost the same whether the phone is quiet or busy, and they leave when the season does. The problem is bursty. A fixed headcount is the wrong tool for a spike, and a missed spike doesn't come back.

What actually works: overflow handling with clear rules

The practical answer is to let an AI receptionist take the overflow. When your lines are busy or your team is on jobs, calls go to it instead of voicemail. It answers right away, asks what's going on, sorts urgent from routine using rules you set, books routine work into your real calendar, and texts the caller a confirmation. Urgent calls get passed straight to your on-call person. It runs on the phone system you already have, such as RingCentral or Twilio, and works with scheduling tools like ServiceTitan and Housecall Pro. The point isn't replacing your team. It's making sure the 90th call of the day gets the same answer as the 5th. See how it works on our AI Virtual Receptionist page.

Where we draw a hard line

It follows your rules about what counts as an emergency, and it never makes a safety or diagnostic judgment of its own. For anything involving danger, such as a gas smell, a fire, or a medical issue, the right rule is to tell the caller to contact emergency services first, and we help you write that rule down. It doesn't quote jobs it isn't authorized to quote, and it always hands off to a person when a caller asks for one.

The bottom line

If your phone only fails when you're busiest, the fix isn't working harder during those weeks. It's making sure overflow has somewhere to go.
