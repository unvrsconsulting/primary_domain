The Visit That Starts Without the Information You Needed

Connor Bolin · Oct 8, 2026 · 6 min read

A technician drives to a job and finds out the water heater is a model he doesn't have parts for. A new patient arrives and the insurance details are missing. A cleaner shows up to a home that's twice the size the booking said. Nobody did anything wrong. The information that would have changed the visit simply never made it from the customer to the person doing the work.

Most of what we cover is about winning or keeping customers. This one is about not wasting the visit you already won. It's a smaller, quieter leak, but it shows up in almost every appointment-based business.

The math most businesses haven't run

Here's an illustrative example, so swap in your own numbers:

  • Say a service company runs about 20 jobs a week, and 15% need a second trip because something wasn't known on the first one: the wrong part, a blocked access point, or a bigger scope than described. That's 3 repeat trips a week.
  • If each wasted trip costs around $90 in labor, fuel, and truck time, that's $270 a week, or roughly $14,000 a year.
  • That doesn't count the schedule slot you couldn't give to a paying job, or the customer who had to take another morning off work.

Count your own repeat trips for a month before trusting any of these figures. The exact numbers vary by trade, but most owners find the count is higher than they assumed.

Why the details go missing

The first call or web form captures what the customer thought to mention, which is rarely what the technician needs. Nobody follows up to ask for a photo of the unit, the model number, the gate code, or the pet in the yard, because the office is already on the next call. The details get discovered at the door, which is the most expensive place to discover them. This is a process gap, not a people problem.

What actually works: a short intake request right after booking

Once a job or appointment is booked, an automated message goes out in your wording: a few plain questions tailored to the job type, with a simple way to reply with a photo. For a water heater, that might be a picture of the label and the space around it. For a cleaning, the number of bedrooms and bathrooms. For a dental or vet visit, a link to your own forms. Answers get attached to the booking in ServiceTitan, Jobber, or Housecall Pro, and a gentle reminder goes out if the customer hasn't replied by the day before. It can be built with forms like Jotform and texting through Twilio. See our Lead Follow-Up Automation page for how this fits together.

Where we draw a hard line

Intake is about collecting information, not interpreting it. It never diagnoses a problem from a photo, never promises a price or a fix based on what a customer sends, and every answer is reviewed by your team before it changes the job. Sensitive details are handled carefully: we don't ask customers to text card numbers, and for medical or clinical information at a dental or veterinary practice, the questions stay inside your own approved forms and systems rather than a general text thread.

The bottom line

Wasted visits are a quiet cost, and most of them trace back to information that was easy to ask for and never requested. Collecting it before the visit protects your schedule and your customer's time. It pairs well with the reminders in the cost of a no-show, and you can check whether it's working with our five-number measurement guide.
